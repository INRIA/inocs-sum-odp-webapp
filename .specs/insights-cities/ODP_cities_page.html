The design decisions behind this, in case you want to argue with them.
  • Identical card skeleton, five fixed slots — identity, context, measures, movement, link. Every card fills the same slots in the same order at the same height, so the eye can move between them without re-learning the layout. That is visual comparability. It is deliberately not numerical comparability.
  • Nothing shares a scale across cards. No common axis, no bar length that means the same thing on two cards, no rank badge, no score. The measures bar shows push/pull proportion within a city, never a length you can lay against the next card. This is the single design rule that keeps the page from becoming a league table by accident.
  • Sorted alphabetically, and the count says so. Sort order is an argument. Alphabetical makes no claim.
  • "Results not published yet" is a designed state, not a gap. Same card height, same slot, a hatched background so it reads as intentional. A city with no indicators still shows its population, its area and its measures — so the card is never empty and never looks broken.
  • Improved / declined, not positive / negative. The label follows the indicator's policy-aligned direction, so a fall in private-car share reads as an improvement. The arrow and the word carry the meaning; colour only reinforces it.
  • "6 of 11 indicator groups improved" sits above the lists. Without it, showing three up and two down implies a balance that may not exist. The counts are the honest summary; the named indicators are the detail.
